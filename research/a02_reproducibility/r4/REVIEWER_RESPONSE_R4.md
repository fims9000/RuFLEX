# Research revision R4: point-by-point response to ACDSA 2027 paper #1326 reviews

**Submission:** *When High Confidence Is Not Reproducible* (ACDSA 2027, rejected).  
**Status:** research revision for a possible new venue. These responses do not imply that ACDSA has granted an appeal or invited a revision.

## Reviewer #3: "The authors failed to answer the question posed in the article's title"

The revised paper now defines decision reproducibility a priori, conditional on a fixed training set, model architecture, hyperparameters, preprocessing, label threshold and distribution of retraining randomness. Reproducibility is the probability that a fresh retraining produces the *reference predictor's class*, not the chance that the class is correct. The paper separates three objects: (i) confidence of the operational reference fit, (ii) reproducibility of its decision under repeated fitting and (iii) ground-truth correctness.

Two mathematically sufficient conditions are explicit. A deterministic score-drift condition guarantees unchanged argmax if the reference top-two score gap exceeds twice a valid sup-norm perturbation bound. A binary threshold analogue uses the reference distance to its *actual* class threshold (which need not be 0.5). A probabilistic finite-run certificate uses a one-sided exact binomial lower confidence bound for fixed-reference label replication, adjusted for simultaneous case-level statements inside each dataset-model cell. Under the independent-draw assumption, A05 certified 4,601/5,400 held-out case-model decisions at a target replication probability of at least 0.9, but 184 certified decisions were incorrect. We therefore do not interpret reproducibility as correctness.

**Boundary of the response:** The mathematical margin condition and Clopper-Pearson interval are standard sufficient conditions/statistics, not newly discovered mathematical results. The former requires a genuinely justified score-variation bound; observed differences among a finite set of runs are not an all-runs guarantee. The latter assumes a fixed reference and iid randomized retraining trials, and seed integers do not prove the relevant sampling model. We claim conditional validity, not an unconditional or deployment-wide guarantee.

## Reviewer #4: "only three public datasets and limited model configurations"

The original A01 300 fits, 15 cells, fixed test outcomes and counterexamples are retained without modification. The exploratory A02-A05 extensions add new tasks, alternative model families, score variability analyses, a run-budget study and held-out, exactly matched review comparisons. In A03 (80 correlated model-data-split cells, 2,480 fits) the combined confidence-plus-disagreement rule improved accepted-case risk in 28 cells, tied in 47 and worsened it in five versus confidence-only at **identical batch review counts**. Stronger alternatives include audit-only probability dispersion, ensemble-mean entropy and vote uncertainty; their negative results are reported, not suppressed.

R4 adds A06 as a more operationally meaningful generalization test using four previously unused bundled datasets: Fair survey, RAND HIE, Australian mode choice and ANES96. Across two fixed splits, four model families and 15 auxiliary runs per fixed reference, A06 contains 32 correlated cells, 512 fits and 45,352 held-out case-model predictions. Review thresholds and deterministic tie-handling rules are fixed exclusively from validation, before held-out scoring, and can process one case without access to the whole test batch. The combined score improved accepted-case risk in 17 cells, tied in eight and worsened in seven versus confidence-only; however test coverage differed by up to 3.57 percentage points, preventing a clean single-outcome risk superiority claim. A separate *batch-only*, exactly workload-matched diagnostic showed 18 improvements, 11 ties and three deteriorations.

A particularly informative negative case is Australian mode choice / Decision Tree / split 404 / case 679: the reference decision was wrong even though its confidence and auxiliary-run agreement were both 1.0. The baseline confidence rule reviewed the case under the matched-batch budget, while the combined score allowed it through. Another adverse case is Fair / SGD Logistic / split 303 / case 162: incorrect reference label with complete agreement (1.0). These examples illustrate why agreement is not a correctness certificate and why the combined review score may lose useful review slots.

## Methodological transparency and validity limitations

The A02-A06 work was motivated by reviewing the original ACDSA rejection; the new results must be called exploratory rather than retroactive preregistered confirmations. Across split seeds, the same datasets and individuals are reused and cells are dependent; counts of better/worse cells are not independent sign tests. Exact-budget batch selection is transductive, whereas validation-frozen streaming thresholds incur uncontrolled differences in test review coverage. Four of 32 unadjusted exploratory 95% paired bootstrap intervals in A06 excluded zero, all favorably, but no multiplicity-adjusted global superiority is claimed. Mode-choice bootstraps use traveller-level groups; RAND HIE may contain repeated individual observations not fully identified in the packaged table. Historical survey tasks do not constitute deployment, a modern temporal external cohort, clinical validation or a fully specified discrete-choice model.

## Reproducibility package

The accompanying R4 research package includes executable A06 code, a frozen local protocol and SHA-256, case-level validation and held-out scores, calibration thresholds, independent replay verifier, 8 negative/positive unit tests, 2,500-replicate fixed-mask paired bootstrap analyses, failure examples, the reconstructed 11-page manuscript, figures and reproducibility instructions. A06 independent verification re-created all 1,728 policy outcomes from 90,696 saved case rows. The original A01-A05 results are preserved in their separate R3 release.

**Publication-facing conclusion:** The strongest defensible contribution is an experimentally controlled separation of confidence, conditional decision reproducibility and review risk, with formally stated conditional certificates, a fixed operational reference predictor and evidence that disagreement-aware ranking sometimes helps at matched workload. General superiority or a new mathematical theorem is not established. A new submission should be formatted for the chosen venue rather than uploaded as an ACDSA camera-ready revision.
